Where it fails

  • 27 Dec 2019: read Calm, Nifty then fell 29.3% over 13 weeks. Covid: falling crude and US yields counted as easing, and FII selling (+5.7σ by 20 Mar) came only after the read.
  • 08 Jul 2011: read Calm, Nifty then fell 13.6% over 13 weeks. FII flows were flat at the read (−0.1σ). The US downgrade and eurozone stress pushed US yields down, which the model counts as easing; FII selling built later (+1.7σ by late Aug).
  • 26 Dec 2025: read Calm, Nifty then fell 12.4% over 13 weeks. FII selling had started (+0.9σ) but the other signals offset it. Outflows then hit a record: +5.7σ by late March, about ₹1.36 lakh crore over 13 weeks.
  • 17 Feb 2012: read Calm, Nifty then fell 12.1% over 13 weeks. The flow signal pointed the wrong way: FIIs were buying hard (−1.3σ) and stayed net buyers through the fall, so the selloff came from outside the five inputs.
  • 27 Nov 2015: read Calm, Nifty then fell 11.5% over 13 weeks. FII selling was already visible (+1.3σ), but falling crude counted as easing and outweighed it: the same crude blind spot as Covid.

Method

Each signal is its 13-week change (FII: 4-week net flow), z-scored against the prior three years (FII: all history since 2010), using past data only, signed so that higher means more pressure on Indian equities. Pressure is their equal-weight average. Pressure regime: above 0.5 and rising. Catch-up: elevated within 8 weeks and down more than 0.5 in 4 weeks. Grind: above zero otherwise. Calm: at or below zero.

Version note

v2 changed one thing, after the first sanity check: the FII signal went from a 13-week sum z-scored on a rolling three-year window to a 4-week sum z-scored on an expanding window from 2010. Under v1, years of steady selling had become the baseline, so heavy outflows barely registered. All other signals and both thresholds are unchanged, and there will be no further tuning.
